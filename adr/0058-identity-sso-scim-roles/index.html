    ADR-0058: Accounts with scoped roles; OIDC and SAML through checked libraries; SCIM; a guest database role¶

    Status: Accepted (2026-10-07), Week 75 (plan §51). This is the plan's "ADR-022". Builds on ADR-0013 (RLS between organizations) and ADR-0018 (scoped tokens).

    Context¶

    Until now a person was an API token pasted into a browser tab, and each person picked their own role (lead, engineer, tester) for their home page. Plan §51 asks for OIDC sign-in with PKCE and strict issuer, audience and nonce checks; SAML 2.0 "through a maintained library only", tested against signature wrapping, NameID comment injection and unsigned assertions; SCIM provisioning and deprovisioning "so someone leaving the company loses access within minutes"; and organization roles with per-portfolio and per-project overrides that cascade, proved by a two-project variant of the two-tenant test.

    Decision¶

    Accounts. A user belongs to one organization and has an organization role: admin, member, viewer, or none (only what is granted). Overrides give a higher role on a business unit (reaching its projects) or one project. They only raise access, as DefectDojo's product permissions do; to give a contractor one project, their organization role is none. Roles become the token scopes the API already checks, so each route keeps its one require_scope(...): for a person, the scopes are those of their role on the project the path names (project, scan, Issue, acceptance, engagement, import, ...), else of their organization role. Organization-wide changes need an organization role; someone limited to some projects can read only the project list, the cross-project Issues list, saved views and their own things. A token a person makes acts as them, within its scopes and their role when it is used. Users are user:<id> actors everywhere people were (assignment, mentions, approvers); tokens from before accounts keep working as before.

    Sessions. A sign-in creates a session: an HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, Secure cookie with a random secret, stored as its SHA-256; 12 hours at most, 2 hours idle. A change made with a session needs its CSRF token in X-Bulwark-CSRF, which only this site's pages can read (/auth/session, over CORS with an explicit origin list and credentials). Signing out, turning someone off and SCIM deprovisioning end sessions at once. Evidence files are served with a sandboxing CSP, so a file that runs script on the API's host cannot use the cookie.

    OIDC is ours on PyJWT: the protocol is small and the checks are the point, so they are written out and tested one by one (asymmetric algorithms only; exact issuer; audience and azp; expiry and issue time with a minute's skew; the nonce; PKCE S256; one-time state; HTTPS to the provider except on this machine). The provider's own description is read when it is saved and must name the issuer configured.

    SAML is pysaml2 with xmlsec1: assertions must be signed, unsolicited responses are refused, and audience, destination and validity are pysaml2's checks. Bulwark never reads a SAML response itself. The attack tests assert why each forgery is refused, so a test cannot pass on an unrelated error.

    Who signs in is decided by the provider's stable subject (OIDC sub, SAML NameID), linked to the account on the first sign-in: a second account at the provider with the same email cannot take someone's place, and an email changed at the provider does not move them. The email must be in one of the provider's domains and verified (OIDC), unless the provider is trusted for emails (Entra ID sends no email_verified). A first sign-in creates the account only when the provider is set to.

    SCIM (/scim/v2/Users) takes only a token with the scim scope, made for the provider and belonging to no one. New people are viewers unless the provider sends a role. active: false or DELETE turns the person off in the same request; DELETE also hides them from the provider while keeping them for the audit trail, and provisioning them again restores the same account.

    Projects a person may not see are hidden by Postgres, not only by the app. Their requests run as the bulwark_guest role (SET LOCAL ROLE), whose restrictive RLS policies on assets and every table that names a project -- directly, or through an Issue, a scan, an engagement or an engagement test -- keep only the projects in app.project_scope. The service roles may switch to it but do not inherit it (WITH INHERIT FALSE, SET TRUE, Postgres 16), because a policy applies to every role whose privileges a session has.

    Alternatives tried¶

    • The same restrictive policies for every session ("all projects, or this list"). Postgres estimated that such a policy let 2 of 200 projects through, and plans through assets collapsed: at a million Occurrences the organization-wide "past SLA" metric went from a 7 ms median to 224 ms (its list of the ten most overdue Issues took 666 ms under EXPLAIN ANALYZE). Limited to the guest role, the policies cost nothing for everyone else.
    • JIT compilation. Turned off per transaction: on large counts it took 116 of 153 ms, and it was costing every large count before this week too (counting 100,000 Issues: 93 ms with, 44 ms without).

    Consequences¶

    • Postgres 16 or later from migration 0048. xmlsec1 in the API image and in CI.
    • Google Workspace, Entra ID and Okta are covered by standard OIDC (and Entra's missing claim by the trust setting), but only Keycloak was signed in through here.
    • A person limited to some projects cannot make organization-wide changes, including bulk changes on the cross-project Issues list; on their own projects' pages they can.
    • The first admin is made with bulwark users add --role admin, or by the shared token.
    • The WebSocket for live scans authenticates with a pasted token only; signed-in browsers fall back to polling, which the scan page already does.
