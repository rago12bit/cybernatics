    Sign-in, people and access¶

    People sign in to Bulwark with your organization's identity provider (OIDC or SAML), are added and removed by it over SCIM, and have a role in the organization, with higher roles on some business units or projects when they need them. Scripts and CI use API tokens. Design and reasoning: ADR-0058.

    Roles¶

    Role May
    Admin everything a member may, and run the organization: people and roles, sign-in, SCIM, rules
    Member scan, triage, import reports, request fixes and risk acceptances
    Viewer read
    Only granted projects see nothing but the business units and projects given to them

    A role on a business unit or a project only adds to the organization role. A business unit's role covers all its projects. To give a contractor one project, make their organization role "Only granted projects" and give them that project. They will see that project, the Issues list (with only that project in it), saved views and their own things; dashboards, reports and other organization-wide pages are for people with an organization role.

    Change roles in Settings → People and access. Every change is in the audit log.

    The first admin¶

    Before anyone can sign in, make the first admin on the server:

    bulwark users add --org-id <organization id> --email you@example.com --role admin

    They then sign in with your identity provider and set up everything else in the app. (The server's shared BULWARK_API_TOKEN can do all of this too.)

    Server settings¶

    Variable What
    BULWARK_PUBLIC_URL the web app's address, where a finished sign-in lands (e.g. https://bulwark.example.com)
    BULWARK_API_PUBLIC_URL the API's address, which identity providers call back (e.g. https://api.bulwark.example.com)
    BULWARK_CORS_ORIGINS the web app's origin, exactly; sign-in cookies need it
    BULWARK_SESSION_COOKIE_SECURE true (default). Set false only for plain-HTTP local development

    Postgres 16 or later is needed (migration 0048).

    Single sign-on with OIDC¶

    In Settings → Sign-in and provisioning, choose Add an OIDC provider and give its issuer, the client id and secret your provider gave Bulwark, and the email domains it signs in. Give the provider the redirect URI the screen shows. Bulwark reads the provider's own description when you save, and refuses one that names a different issuer.

    Provider Issuer Notes
    Google Workspace https://accounts.google.com an OAuth client of type "Web application"
    Microsoft Entra ID https://login.microsoftonline.com/<tenant id>/v2.0 Entra sends no email_verified: tick "Trust the provider's emails"
    Okta https://<your org>.okta.com (or a custom authorization server's) an OIDC web app
    Keycloak https://<host>/realms/<realm> a confidential client; PKCE method S256

    Every sign-in uses PKCE. The provider's ID token is checked strictly -- its signature, issuer, audience, expiry and the nonce of that sign-in -- and anything else is refused.

    Single sign-on with SAML¶

    Choose Add a SAML provider and paste your provider's metadata (or its entity ID, sign-in URL and signing certificate). Give your provider Bulwark's entity ID and assertion consumer service URL from the screen, or our metadata at the entity ID's address. Your provider must sign its assertions: Bulwark refuses unsigned ones, and responses it did not ask for.

    Who is who¶

    The first time someone signs in, Bulwark remembers the provider's stable id for them. After that, that id decides who they are, so another account at the provider with the same email cannot become them. Their email must be in one of the provider's domains. If A first sign-in creates the account is off, only people already added (by an admin or by SCIM) can sign in.

    Sessions last at most 12 hours, and end after 2 hours unused.

    Provisioning with SCIM¶

    Make a SCIM token in Settings → Sign-in and provisioning and give it, with the SCIM base URL shown there, to your identity provider's provisioning settings. Your provider then adds people (as viewers, unless it sends a role), changes them, and turns them off. Someone it turns off is signed out everywhere in the same moment, and every API token they made stops working.

    Supported: Users (create, read, list with filter=userName eq "...", replace, patch, delete). Not supported: Groups and bulk requests.

    API tokens¶

    Settings → My API tokens makes a token for CI or a script. It acts as you, with the scopes you give it -- never more than your role allows at the time it is used -- and stops working when you are turned off. It is shown once.

    API¶

    GET  /auth/sso/discover?email=           the provider for an email's domain
GET  /auth/sso/{id}/start?next=          start a sign-in
GET  /auth/session                       who is signed in, and the CSRF token
POST /auth/logout
GET|POST /v2/users, GET|PATCH /v2/users/{id}, PUT /v2/users/{id}/roles
GET|POST /v2/sso/providers, GET|PATCH|DELETE /v2/sso/providers/{id}, POST .../{id}/test
GET|POST /v2/scim/tokens, DELETE /v2/scim/tokens/{id}
GET|POST /v2/me/tokens, DELETE /v2/me/tokens/{id}
/scim/v2/Users ...                       for your identity provider
